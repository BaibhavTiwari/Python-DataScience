## Monte Carlo simulations (Day 12)

- **Monte Carlo simulation**: A technique that uses repeated random sampling to model the range of possible outcomes for a process or system (such as future stock prices) under defined statistical assumptions.[web:124][web:129]

- **Simulation path / scenario**: One complete, hypothetical evolution of prices over time generated from simulated returns; multiple paths represent different possible futures under the same model.[web:124][web:126]

- **Multiple-path simulation**: Running many simulated price paths in parallel (for example, 500 or 1,000 paths) to obtain a distribution of final wealth or prices instead of a single trajectory.[web:124][web:129]

- **Distribution of final prices**: The set of ending prices across all simulated paths, often visualized as a histogram, used to understand risk and potential outcomes at the horizon (e.g., 1 year).[web:124][web:129]

- **Empirical probability (from simulations)**: The fraction of simulated paths that satisfy a condition (such as final price above 120), interpreted as an estimate of the probability of that event under the chosen model.[web:124][web:129]
